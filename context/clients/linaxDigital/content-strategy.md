# Linax Digital — Content Strategy

> **Purpose.** Page-by-page content brief for `linaxdigital.com` plus an editorial strategy for the blog. Built from `business-context.md`, `personas.md`, `product-marketing-context.md`, `writing-voice.md`, and `site-architecture.md`.
>
> **Posture.** This is an internal strategy doc, so it's structured. The copy that ships from this plan must follow `writing-voice.md` — plainspoken Everyman, no jargon, contractions always, no AI-ese.
>
> **Two operating principles for every page:**
>
> 1. **One job per page.** Don't ask the visitor to do three things. Ask for one. Make the next thing easy.
> 2. **Every page has a path to Calendly.** Site-architecture §6.4 sets a minimum of four Calendly touchpoints. Every page brief here meets that bar.

---

## 1. Strategy at a glance

**Primary content goal:** Convert search and referral traffic into booked consults. The Free Digital Presence Audit is the secondary capture for visitors who aren't ready to book a call yet.

**Secondary goal:** Earn local SEO authority for SWFL service-business search terms ("digital marketing agency Cape Coral," "local SEO Fort Myers," etc.) so prospects find Linax Digital on their own.

**Audience reality check.** The reader is busy, skeptical, and has been pitched by agencies before. They're not looking to be impressed. They're looking for someone they can trust. Every page should respect that — short copy, plain words, real proof.

**What we won't do:**

- No "Contact us for a quote" friction. Pricing is a differentiator. Show it.
- No technical name-dropping. Nobody hires an agency because it uses Next.js.
- No urgency manipulation. No "don't miss out," no countdown timers.
- No three-question hooks ("Tired of bad rankings? Struggling for leads? Ready to grow?"). One is fine. Three is malpractice.
- No AI/jargon talk on the homepage hero. AI is how we work, not what we sell.

## 3. Page-by-page content briefs

### 3.1 Homepage (/)

**Job-to-be-done.** A South FL service-business owner lands here from search, an ad, or a referral. In 30 seconds they need to know: who this is, who it's for, what it does, what it costs, why it's different, and how to start. Every section moves them toward Calendly or the audit.

**Personas.** Mike (primary), James, Dr. Kevin, Mitchells. Designed for the established operator first. Sofia and Rachel will read it but it's not built around them.

**Conversion goals.** Primary: Book a free consult (Calendly). Secondary: Free Digital Presence Audit lead capture.

**Key messages to land (in order of priority):**

1. Local agency for South FL service businesses — websites, SEO, ads, reputation.
2. You talk to the person doing the work. No handoffs, no account managers.
3. Pricing is on the website. No quote runaround.
4. Free audit before you ever pay anything.
5. Built on modern tools — translates to faster, sharper work for the same money.

**Proof points to use:**

- Free initial audit (active, ready to publish).
- 2:1 ROAS guarantee on Ads (active, ready to publish).
- Month-to-month on SEO, Ads, and Reputation Management (active).
- Founder direct — name, photo, credibility line ("I build the sites, I run the campaigns, I answer the phone").
- Numeric proof points are placeholders only until verified — do not publish unverified stats.

**Section order (refines site-architecture §5.1):**

1. **Hero** — One-line value prop + primary CTA (Book free consult) + secondary CTA (Free audit). Founder photo or SWFL environmental shot.
2. **Trust bar** — Logo strip if available, otherwise vertical mentions ("HVAC · Roofing · Charter · Cabinetry · Sod"). Reads as "we work with people like you."
3. **Problem agitation** — Three or four short bullets in customer language. "Your phone isn't ringing the way it should." "Your reviews are scattered across five places." "You've paid an agency before and have nothing to show for it." Land it without rhetorical-question stacking.
4. **Services overview** — Four cards: Websites, Local SEO, Ads, Reputation Management. One outcome line per card. "Most popular" badge on Reputation Management.
5. **The differentiator block** — "You talk to the person doing the work." Founder photo + 60–80 words. The single most important block on the page.
6. **Results / proof** — Stat strip or short quote row. Use only verified numbers. If none verified yet, use the guarantees instead (free audit, ROAS guarantee, month-to-month).
7. **Process** — 4 steps (Free call → Audit & plan → Build & launch → Grow & optimize). Compact teaser; deeper version on /process.
8. **Pricing teaser** — One row per service, starting price, link to /pricing. The transparency is the message.
9. **FAQ** — 6 items. Cost, contracts, timelines, "how are you different," what's included, what happens after the call.
10. **Final CTA** — Warm dark section. "Free 30-minute call. No pitch deck." Calendly button.
11. **Footer.**

**SEO keywords.** Confirms site-architecture §8.

- Primary: `digital marketing agency Cape Coral`
- Secondary: `digital marketing Fort Myers`, `marketing agency Southwest Florida`, `local marketing agency SWFL`
- Title: `Digital Marketing Agency in Cape Coral, FL — Linax Digital`
- Meta description: pricing transparency + free audit + founder-led, under 155 chars.

**Voice notes.** This is the page where the Everyman tone matters most. Hemingway brevity in the hero. Vonnegut warmth in the differentiator. Twain front-porch cadence in the FAQ. No "comprehensive solutions." No "we partner with you on your journey." Test: would Mike actually read past the hero without rolling his eyes?

**Internal linking priorities.**

- Hero CTA → Calendly + /audit
- Services cards → 4 service pages (Reputation gets visual emphasis)
- Pricing teaser → /pricing
- Differentiator block → /about
- Process teaser → /process
- FAQ → /faq

### 3.4 /services/seo (Local SEO)

**Job-to-be-done.** Convince a skeptical reader that local SEO works, that 90 days is a realistic horizon, and that the $600 + $300/mo structure is fair.

**Personas.** Mike (primary — burned by SEO before), Dr. Kevin (analytical), James (modernization), Marcus (multi-location).

**Conversion goals.** Primary: Calendly. Secondary: Free audit (the audit includes an SEO review).

**Key messages:**

- Local SEO is how you show up when someone in your service area searches for what you do.
- Two parts: a one-time foundation ($600) and ongoing work ($300/mo).
- Foundation includes keyword research, on-page mapping, citations with consistent NAP, foundation backlinks, GBP setup.
- Ongoing includes content (4 blog posts/mo), GBP activity, backlink building, press releases.
- Honest timeline: 3–6 months to see compounding results. Some quick wins in 30–60 days (citation cleanup, GBP optimization).

**Proof points:**

- "We use Ahrefs for keyword research" — fine to name the tool, it signals seriousness without being jargon.
- Free audit reveals current SEO state before any commitment.
- Month-to-month on the ongoing — cancel anytime.

**Section order:**

1. **Hero** — "Show up when your customers search for you." Subhead: 3–6 months of compounding work, no lock-in.
2. **What "local SEO" actually means** — Plain-language explainer. NAP, GBP, citations, backlinks — defined in human terms.
3. **What's in the foundation ($600 one-time)** — Bulleted list with brief plain-English notes per item.
4. **What's in the ongoing ($300/mo)** — Same treatment.
5. **What you'll see and when** — Honest timeline section. Month 1 (audit + foundation), Month 2–3 (citations + content begin), Month 4–6 (rankings move). This is the trust-builder.
6. **The "I've been burned by SEO before" section** — Direct address. "Most SEO that fails fails because: no measurement, no on-page work, no GBP, just backlinks. Here's how this is different."
7. **Cross-sell** — "Accelerate with Google Ads while SEO compounds."
8. **FAQ** — 4–5 SEO-specific (cost, timeline, what changes if I cancel, do I own the content).
9. **Final CTA** — Free audit emphasis: "Want to see where you actually rank? Free audit takes a day."

**SEO keywords.**

- Primary: `local SEO Cape Coral`
- Secondary: `local SEO Fort Myers`, `Google Business Profile optimization SWFL`, `small business SEO Southwest Florida`
- Long-tail: `how much does local SEO cost`, `local SEO for service businesses`, `GBP optimization Florida`

**Voice notes.** Ogilvy "specifics beat superlatives." No "industry-leading SEO." Use real numbers when available. Address skepticism directly — Mike's voice is loud here.

**Internal linking priorities.**

- /services/ads (acceleration pairing)

### 3.5 /services/ads (Google & Meta Ads)

**Job-to-be-done.** Reassure a reader that ads can work for their business specifically, explain what "% of ad spend" means without weaseling, and lead with the ROAS guarantee.

**Personas.** Mike, Mitchells (peak season), Dr. Kevin, Marcus.

**Conversion goals.** Primary: Calendly. Secondary: Free audit (with ad-account review when applicable).

**Key messages:**

- Google Ads (search, LSA, performance max, display) and Meta Ads (Facebook, Instagram).
- $300/mo minimum, or 10% of ad spend, whichever is greater. Capped at $2,000/mo in management fees.
- 2:1 ROAS guarantee — if return on ad spend drops below 2:1, no management fee that month.
- We write the copy, build the audiences, manage the campaigns, and report monthly.
- You own the ad accounts. If we part ways, you keep them.

**Proof points:**

- ROAS guarantee — the strongest proof point on the page.
- Account ownership — answers a real anxiety.
- Free audit can include current ad-account review if they're already running ads.

**Section order:**

1. **Hero** — "Pay only when ads pay you back." Subhead: 2:1 ROAS guarantee in plain language.
2. **What we run** — Google search, LSA, PMax, display, and Meta. Two short paragraphs, not a brochure.
3. **What we do, exactly** — Copy, audiences, build, launch, manage, report. Bullets.
4. **The ROAS guarantee** — Standalone block. Full plain-language explanation. "If you put in $1,000 and don't get $2,000 back, you don't pay us management fees that month. Ad spend itself isn't refunded — that's Google's spend, not ours — but our fee is on us until the math works."
5. **Pricing** — $300 floor or 10% of spend, capped at $2,000. Add a worked example: "$3,000/mo ad spend = $300 management. $20,000/mo ad spend = $2,000 management. $50,000/mo = still $2,000."

7. **Honest timeline** — 1–2 weeks to launch, 30–60 days for true performance read.

## 4. Blog editorial strategy

### 4.1 Why a blog at all

Three reasons, in priority order:

1. **Local SEO authority.** SEO sub-page sells the service. The blog is what makes the SEO service visibly work for Linax Digital itself. Posts that rank for SWFL service-business questions are proof-of-concept.
2. **Lead-magnet flow.** Every post anchors a CTA to the Free Audit. Search traffic → useful answer → captured lead.
3. **Sales-call deflection.** Common objections turn into linkable answers. "Read this and let me know if you still want to talk."

The blog is searchable-first. Shareable is a bonus, not the goal. Don't chase virality. Chase rankings for `[service] in [SWFL city]` and the questions Mike actually types into Google at 9pm.

### 4.4 Hub-and-spoke linking plan

**Primary linking model:**

```
Service page (HUB) ←→ Pillar page (HUB) ←→ Cluster posts (SPOKES)
                                            ↓
                                        /audit (lead magnet)
```

**Concrete linking rules:**

1. **Every blog post links to:**
  - Its pillar page (or directly to the service page if no pillar exists yet).
  - The Free Audit, in-content and at the post footer.
  - At least one related blog post (encourages session depth).
2. **Pillar pages link to:**
  - All cluster posts in their cluster.
  - The corresponding service page (above the fold and in the conclusion).
  - The Free Audit at the end.
3. **Service pages link to:**
  - The pillar page for their pillar (in a "Want to learn more?" section near the bottom).
  - 2–3 supporting blog posts that demonstrate authority.
4. **Free Audit page (/audit) is inbound-only.**
  - Every blog post sends traffic there.
  - The audit page does not link back out (except in footer). It's a terminus.
5. **Cross-pillar internal links** — Reinforce the "they fit together" message:
  - SEO posts link to relevant Reputation post (reviews feed local rankings).
  - Ads posts link to relevant Reputation post (social proof for ad creative).
  - Website posts link to relevant SEO post (technical foundation).

### 4.5 Editorial cadence

**First 90 days:**

- Week 1: Publish 3 pillar pages.
- Weeks 2–4: Publish 4 launch posts (priority: posts #1, #4, #7, #11 — one per pillar, TOFU).
- Months 2–3: Publish 8 remaining launch posts at 1/week.

**Steady state (post-launch):**

- 1 post per week, alternating between pillars.
- 1 pillar-page refresh per quarter (keeps anchors fresh, signals to search that the hub is maintained).

**SEO ongoing service includes 4 posts/mo per business-context.** That cadence is for client work, not the Linax blog itself. The Linax blog runs at 1/week — fewer, sharper, longer.

### 4.6 What we won't write about

- **AI as a topic.** AI is how the work gets done, not what we sell. Anti-persona reading, off-brand for Everyman voice.
- **Generic agency content.** "10 marketing trends for 2026" lists, "Why your business needs digital marketing" softball pieces. Skippable, low-credibility, easily generated by anyone — actively harms the brand.
- **Industry hype reactions.** Algorithm changes, platform drama, AI takes. The audience doesn't read it and doesn't care.
- **Listicle padding.** "27 reasons your business needs SEO." Nobody reads them. They make the brand look generic.

The bar for every post: **would a charter captain or cabinet maker actually read this without rolling their eyes?** If no, kill it.

## 5. Content production checklist (per page or post)

Before any page or post ships, verify:

- Opens with something concrete — a scene, number, observation, or real question. Never "In today's digital landscape..."
- Average sentence under 20 words.
- Contractions throughout.
- No words from the Never-Use list (delve, leverage, unlock, empower, comprehensive, solutions as filler, seamless, cutting-edge, robust, etc.).
- At most one exclamation point on the page (and only if it earns it).
- Ends on an idea or calm invitation, not a "Contact us today!"
- At least one path to Calendly visible without scrolling past 2 viewport heights.
- Free Audit referenced if the visitor isn't ready to book a call.
- All claims defensible on a sales call (Ogilvy rule).
- Would a SWFL service-business owner read it without rolling their eyes (Mike test)?

If any answer is no, rewrite.

## 6. Open items before content production starts

These need resolution before serious copy production begins:

1. **Founder writing samples** — Per business-context §7, founder will provide 2–3 paragraphs of personal writing as voice anchors. Get these before drafting the homepage hero or /about story.
2. **Verified proof numbers** — Stop using placeholder stats (340% leads, 50+ businesses, 4.9★, 2-week launch). Either verify or remove. Placeholder numbers in shipped copy actively damage trust.
3. **First case study** — At least one full case study with real numbers and a client quote should be ready by month 2. Without it, /case-studies stays a stub and /about loses meaningful proof.
4. **Tagline** — Per business-context §7, tagline is defined after site copy is drafted. Decide whether the homepage hero copy itself can do tagline duty, or whether a separate Fraunces line is needed in the footer/logo lockup.
5. **Blog launch trigger** — Don't publish the blog until pillar pages + first 4 cluster posts are written. A half-empty blog signals neglect. A 7-post launch with a clear pillar structure signals seriousness.
