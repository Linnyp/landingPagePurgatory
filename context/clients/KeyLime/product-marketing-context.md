## Product Overview

**One-liner:** KeyLime Marketing is a managed digital marketing system for local service businesses — one team, one platform, one monthly price — combining a replicable HighLevel-powered backbone with specialized work (SEO, paid ads, custom websites) where it counts.

**What it does:** KeyLime gives home services and beauty operators a complete, working marketing system without making them assemble or operate the stack themselves. Every client gets a dedicated HighLevel sub-account that runs lead capture, CRM, missed-call text-back, booking, reviews, follow-up, and customer messaging in one place. KeyLime configures and runs the system; the client uses the parts they need (inbox, CRM, reporting) and gets back to running the business. Tiered subscriptions (Foundation, Growth, Expansion) bundle platform features and, at higher tiers, specialized work like SEO foundations, custom websites, and content programs — with add-ons and custom-scoped engagements available when a business is ready.

**Product category:** Managed digital marketing system for local service businesses. How customers search: "marketing for HVAC," "salon marketing software," "local SEO Naples," "review management for contractors," "marketing automation for med spas," "all-in-one marketing platform for small business."

**Product type:** B2B service/SaaS hybrid. Replicable platform infrastructure plus done-for-you delivery. Sold as a managed subscription, not a self-serve SaaS product the client operates.

**Business model:**
- **Foundation — $99/mo:** Site-builder website (1 landing page), CRM, Reputation Management, Unified Inbox, dedicated Twilio number, A2P 10DLC registration, HighLevel sub-account, baseline workflows.
- **Growth — $195/mo:** Foundation plus Speed-to-Lead (missed-call text-back, form auto-response, pre-qualification), Booking, Chatbots, Appointment Reminders, Birthday Campaigns, SEO Foundation, 3–5 page builder site.
- **Expansion — $495/mo:** Growth plus Lead Nurture, Rewards/loyalty, GBP management, quarterly Database Reactivation, custom Webflow site (5–10 pages), monthly blog content.
- **One-time setup fee:** $200 per engagement. Covers platform provisioning, A2P 10DLC registration, baseline configuration, and the written gameplan deliverable.
- **Add-ons (published pricing):** Voice Agents, Lead Nurture, Rewards, Database Reactivation (one-time campaigns), GMB management on lower tiers, additional automations and chatbots.
- **Custom-quoted specialized work:** SEO programs beyond foundation, ongoing content production, Google and Meta ads management, custom Webflow builds beyond included pages, large bespoke workflow projects.
- All tiers are month-to-month with no long-term lock-in. Setup fee billed once at engagement start.

## Target Audience

**Target companies:** Local service businesses in two industries that share the same operational shape (repeat customers, appointment-driven revenue, reliance on local reputation, strong need for fast lead response):
- **Home services.** Remodeling, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, garage doors, epoxy and flooring, pest control, landscaping, pressure washing, pool service, and similar trades.
- **Beauty and personal services.** Salons, barbershops, med spas, lash and brow studios, nail salons, massage and body work, and similar appointment-based personal services.

Typical revenue range: $300K–$5M. Established or growing operators who know their trade but don't have the time, team, or interest to operate a marketing stack themselves. Initial sales focus is Southwest Florida (Lee, Collier, Charlotte counties); the system is geography-independent and serves qualifying businesses nationwide.

**Decision-makers:** Business owner or operator (same person in most cases). In multi-station beauty businesses, that's the salon/spa owner. In home services, it's typically the founder-operator or general manager. Decisions made on gut + referrals + visible results. Budget-conscious but will invest when the math is clear. Secondary influencer: office manager, front-desk lead, or operations coordinator who researches and champions tools before bringing them to the owner.

**Primary use case:** Replace the patchwork of disconnected tools and inconsistent follow-up with one managed system that captures every lead, responds instantly, books and reminds, generates reviews, and reactivates dormant customers — without the owner having to operate any of it.

**Jobs to be done:**
- "Stop losing leads to whoever called the next business when I missed the phone."
- "Get reviews coming in steadily so my Google listing actually reflects the quality of my work."
- "Cut down on no-shows without me or my front desk chasing every appointment."
- "Have one inbox for customer messages instead of five tabs and three apps."
- "Bring back the customers who already paid me once."
- "Look as credible online as I actually am in person."

**Use cases:**
- HVAC owner replaces a missed-call problem and a stale GBP with Growth — missed-call text-back, automated review requests, online booking, and SEO Foundation — and pays for the subscription in recovered jobs the first month.
- Med spa owner moves from a $250/mo SaaS stack (separate CRM, separate texting tool, separate review software, separate booking page) to Expansion — same money, more done, no more juggling logins.
- Solo beauty operator just past her LLC milestone starts on Foundation — landing page, booking, CRM, reviews — for $99/mo to look professional without taking on overhead she can't carry yet.
- Multi-location plumbing operator deploys Expansion per location, with per-location GBP management and rolled-up reporting, and adds Google Ads as custom-quoted specialized work.
- Legacy roofing company (25+ years) modernizes with Expansion — custom Webflow site, content program, full lifecycle automation — to compete with younger digital-native competitors without losing the reputation built on handshake referrals.

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** Local service business owners are losing leads, revenue, and customer relationships to a patchwork of disconnected tools, inconsistent follow-up, and a digital presence that doesn't reflect the quality of their work. They know it's costing them business. They don't have time to fix it, don't want to become marketers, and don't trust most agencies to fix it for them.

**Why current alternatives fall short:**
- **DIY marketing.** Sporadic, inconsistent, and outside their skillset. The owner's nephew posting on Instagram twice a month isn't a marketing strategy.
- **Generalist local agencies.** Template sites, vague deliverables, junior or offshore execution, no transparency, and an account manager who isn't the person doing the work. Most prospects have already tried this and been burned.
- **Disconnected SaaS stack.** Five subscriptions (separate CRM, separate review tool, separate booking software, separate texting platform, separate email system) that don't talk to each other. The owner pays for capability they never fully use.
- **Doing nothing / word-of-mouth only.** "Works" just enough to rationalize not changing. Leaves the business invisible to customers who search rather than ask for referrals.
- **Pure SaaS platforms (HubSpot, Mindbody, GHL self-serve, etc.).** Require the owner to learn and operate them. That's exactly the time and attention the owner doesn't have.
- **Angi / HomeAdvisor / shared-lead platforms.** Expensive, shared with 3–5 competitors, and not owned traffic.

**What it costs them:**
- Lost jobs and appointments to whoever called the next business after the missed call.
- A Google listing that doesn't reflect reality — 50 reviews when competitors have 300, or stale 2-year-old reviews that look like the business is dead.
- 15–20% no-show rates eating revenue out of an otherwise booked calendar.
- Old quotes and dormant customers worth thousands in lifetime value never followed up on.
- Hours per week manually doing what an automation should do.
- Money paid every month for SaaS tools the team never logs into.

**Emotional tension:** "I've been burned before. I know I need this, but I don't know who to trust, and I can't afford to waste money again." Layered under that: "I don't want to feel stupid in front of someone who talks in jargon I can't follow." And underneath that: "If I buy another tool, I'm the one who's going to have to learn it. I don't have time for that."

## Competitive Landscape

**Direct — generalist local marketing agencies (in SWFL and broadly).** Same buyer, same problem framing, different solution shape. Fall short because they sell a list of services rather than a working system, ship template sites on dated platforms, hide pricing behind "contact us" forms, and use account managers as a buffer between the client and the people doing the work. The KeyLime prospect has often already hired one — and is leaving precisely because it didn't produce results.

**Direct — HighLevel agency resellers and "all-in-one" marketing agencies.** Same platform stack, similar pitch. Fall short because most are pure agency-on-top-of-platform with no tier discipline, no industry specialization, no published pricing structure, and no scaffolding for clients to grow into. Sells the platform; doesn't sell a managed system.

**Secondary — vertical-specific SaaS platforms (Mindbody, Vagaro, Boulevard, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, Jobber).** Same buyer industries, different shape. Fall short because they're software products the owner has to learn, configure, and operate. Strong at one part of the workflow (booking, dispatch, payments) but leave gaps in reviews, follow-up, and marketing — which the owner then fills with more SaaS subscriptions. KeyLime runs the marketing system around whichever operations tool the owner already uses.

**Secondary — national franchise marketing agencies.** Different buyer relationship, same broad problem. Fall short because they lack local market knowledge, charge premium prices for cookie-cutter output, and don't deliver a managed platform — they coordinate vendors who execute pieces.

**Indirect — doing nothing / word-of-mouth only.** The single biggest competitor for most prospects. "Working well enough" is the hardest competitor to beat. Falls short because it isn't scalable, leaves the business invisible to search-first customers, and is fragile to any market or seasonal shift.

**Indirect — DIY tools (Squarespace + Mailchimp + Google Forms + a Linktree).** Cheaper-looking on paper but loads the owner with operational work they're already drowning in. Falls short because the owner is the bottleneck.

**Key competitive moat:**
1. **A complete system, not a checklist of services.** One managed system at one monthly price — vs. the assembled stack the buyer is comparing against.
2. **Service/SaaS hybrid economics.** Replicable platform features powered by HighLevel make real marketing infrastructure affordable for $300K-revenue businesses, not just $5M ones. Lower tiers are real working systems, not stripped-down demos.
3. **Industry focus on home services and beauty.** Vertical-tuned workflows, language, integrations, and benchmarks — not a generic template adapted on the fly.
4. **Plain-English communication.** Owners are sold the outcome ("phones ring more, no-shows drop, reviews keep coming in") not the feature acronym soup.
5. **Built for growth in both directions.** The same client can start at Foundation, move up to Expansion with added paid ads, and contract back if conditions change — same platform, same team, no rebuild.

## Switching Dynamics

**Push (frustrations driving them away from current solution):**
- Paying multiple SaaS subscriptions that don't talk to each other and add up to more than a managed-service tier would cost.
- Paying an agency monthly with unclear ROI, no transparency on what's being done, and no defined system to point at.
- Missed calls turning into competitor jobs while the owner is on a different job.
- No-show rates eating revenue and the calendar.
- Reviews trickling in too slowly while competitors stack up hundreds.
- Old quotes and dormant customers piling up unaddressed.

**Pull (what attracts them to KeyLime):**
- One monthly price for a managed system instead of a stack of SaaS subscriptions or an opaque retainer.
- Tier structure that's readable in 30 seconds and right-sized to where the business is.
- "We run it for you" — no platform to learn, no setup to configure.
- Industry-specific tuning for home services and beauty (not a generic template).
- Month-to-month — no long-term lock-in on the core subscription.
- Free discovery call and digital presence review as a no-risk entry point.

**Habit (what keeps them stuck with current approach):**
- The existing agency relationship — switching feels like work and risk, even when it isn't producing.
- Sunk-cost thinking on SaaS tools they've already paid for and "should be using."
- "At least I know what I'm getting" inertia.
- Word-of-mouth working "well enough" to rationalize doing nothing.
- Past agency disappointments — easier to stay than risk another bad experience.

**Anxiety (what worries them about switching):**
- "What if this doesn't work either?" (past agency trauma)
- "Am I going to have to learn a new platform on top of everything else?"
- "What about my existing booking software / customer list / phone number?"
- "Will my reviews and customer data port over cleanly?"
- "What's A2P 10DLC and do I have to deal with it?" (answer: no — KeyLime does)
- "What happens if I want to cancel? Do I lose everything?"

## Goals

**Business goal:** Establish KeyLime Marketing as the default managed marketing system for local service businesses in home services and beauty — first in Southwest Florida (where in-market presence creates the local edge), then nationally as the geography-independent system proves out. Build a recurring revenue base across the three tiers, with add-on attach and tier upgrades driving expansion revenue inside the client base.

**Conversion strategy:** The site runs a layered conversion funnel, not a single "book a call" funnel. Different pages serve different stages of buyer intent, and each page has its own intent-matched primary CTA. The discovery-call CTA is persistent in the header and footer on every page, but is *not* the primary CTA on most pages — using it everywhere dulls it and pattern-matches to every other local agency.

**Primary conversion action (top of funnel — low commitment):** Calculator entry.
- **Missed-call revenue calculator** is the homepage primary CTA — a 60-second, three-input calculator that lets the prospect quantify their own problem before KeyLime says a word about itself.
- **Industry-tuned calculator variants** live on industry pages (HVAC, salon, plumbing, etc.) with vertical-specific defaults, benchmarks, and labels.
- **Stack cost calculator** is the primary CTA on comparison pages ("KeyLime + Mindbody," "KeyLime + Housecall Pro," "KeyLime + Vagaro," and similar) — prospect tallies what their current stack of point tools costs each month and compares against KeyLime's all-in price.

**Secondary conversion action (middle of funnel — email-gated):** Field report downloads. Short, opinionated industry reports (working candidates: "Why local HVAC companies lose 30% of their inbound calls," "The real ROI of review automation in beauty"). Email gate feeds the nurture sequence.

**Tertiary conversion action (bottom of funnel — high intent):** Book a discovery call via embedded Calendly. Primary CTA on tier pages and the Contact / Book a Call page; reachable from every page via persistent header/footer. Reserved for prospects who already understand the problem and want to scope the right tier.

**Quaternary conversion action (custom scope):** Quote request flow for custom-scoped specialized work (full SEO programs, Google/Meta Ads management, custom Webflow builds beyond included pages, large bespoke workflow projects).

**Deprecated:** The legacy "Free Digital Presence Audit" lead magnet is replaced by the layered funnel above. Audit-style language ("get a free audit," "we'll review your digital presence") should not appear in CTAs going forward — it pattern-matches to every other local agency and underperforms the calculator on the homepage.

**Hero direction (loss-led):** The homepage hero is the moment a prospect either pattern-matches KeyLime to "another agency" or registers it as something different. The calculator is the pattern-break, so the hero CTA must be the calculator entry — not a generic "Learn more" or a buried "Book a call." Reference frame:
- **H1:** See what missed calls are costing your business.
- **Sub:** Local home services and beauty operators lose thousands a month to leads that called the next business on the list. Punch in three numbers — get yours in 60 seconds.
- **Primary button:** Calculate my missed-call revenue
- **Secondary text link:** See how the system works

Loss aversion outperforms gain framing for the established-operator profile that's been burned by past agencies. The "three numbers / 60 seconds" guardrail defuses the "this is going to be a sales trap" reflex. Avoid generic "Get started," "Get a free audit," or "Book a call" CTAs in the hero.
