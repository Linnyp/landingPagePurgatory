## 2. The Three Steps

**Section header:** A working system in three steps.

**Step 1 — Pick a system.**
Foundation, Growth, or Expansion. Each one's a complete working system the day it goes live — not a stripped-down preview of the next tier up. Prices are on the site. Month-to-month. Move up or down anytime.

**Step 2 — We onboard you in 4 to 8 weeks.**
Discovery, written gameplan, platform setup, workflows built, integrations connected. Single point of contact the whole way through. By week eight, the system is live.

**Step 3 — The system runs. We run the system.**
Your phones get answered faster. Reviews come in steadier. No-shows drop. You use the inbox, the CRM, and the monthly report. We handle everything behind them.

> _Steps mirror the homepage 3-step snapshot (§4) so a visitor arriving from the homepage gets immediate recognition. This page is the expanded version — the detail lives in §3, §4, §5 below, which drill into setup, runtime usage, and what KeyLime runs behind the scenes. Each step still ends on the outcome, not the activity._

## 3. What's Included on Every Account

**Section header:** Every account starts with the same foundation.

**Body (one sentence):**
Some things you need regardless of which system you pick. We build them in so you never have to think about them.

**Bullets:**

- A dedicated business phone number for texts and call routing.
- SMS carrier registration — the legal step that lets your business text customers without getting blocked.
- A private workspace inside our platform. Your contacts, your messages, your workflows, walled off from everyone else's.
- Baseline workflows for review requests, booking confirmations, and customer follow-up, running the day the system goes live.
- A written gameplan tailored to your business — what we'll do, in what order, and why.
- A single point of contact for the entire engagement.

**Closing line (single sentence):**
That's the floor. Each system adds to it.

> _Translates the compliance-and-infrastructure floor (Twilio number, A2P 10DLC, sub-account, baseline workflows) into plain-language outcomes per voice rules — no platform brand names, no acronyms. The "we build them in so you never have to think about them" framing directly addresses the "what about A2P 10DLC, Twilio, all that compliance stuff" objection from the product marketing context. Closing line bridges into §4 — what you actually touch — instead of leaving the reader at infrastructure._

## 4. What You Actually Use

**Section header:** What you actually log into.

**Body (one sentence):**
Three things. The rest runs without you.

**The inbox.**
One place for every customer message — texts, emails, webchat, and Google messages on one screen. Reply from your phone or your laptop.

**The CRM.**
Your contact list with memory. Who they are, when you last talked, where they are in your process, what they bought. Search it like a phone book.

**The monthly report.**
What the system did, what it produced, and where the next opportunity is. Plain English. No acronym wall.

**Closing line:**
No new platform to learn. No certifications to chase. No software to maintain.

> _This section is the single most load-bearing one on the page — it's the answer to "do I have to learn another platform?" (the #2 objection in the product marketing context). Keeping it to three concrete things ("you log into these — that's it") defuses the "you'll be sold something you can't operate" anxiety surfaced in `business-context.md` §3. The "phone book" simile for the CRM is deliberately low-tech — operators recognize it instantly._

### Alt closing line

- **Option B (sharper):** "That's the surface. The rest of the system runs underneath it." — Sets up §5 more directly, but loses the three "no platform / no certifications / no software" beats that explicitly counter the objection.

> _Going with the spec'd version. The three "no X" beats earn their space here because they're the precise reassurances the persona research says owners need to hear out loud._

## 5. What We Do Behind the Scenes

**Section header:** What we run for you.

**Body (one sentence):**
This is the part most agencies sell you as a list of services. We treat it as the work.

**Bullets:**

- Build and tune the workflows that capture leads, request reviews, and follow up automatically.
- Configure the integrations so your booking software, customer list, and phone number talk to each other instead of working in silos.
- Monitor the automations and fix what breaks before you notice.
- Optimize the campaigns inside your system — review pacing, follow-up cadence, reminder timing — based on what's actually working for your business.
- Ship the monthly report so you see results without digging through dashboards.
- Handle carrier compliance, deliverability, and platform updates that would otherwise be your problem.

**Closing line:**
When something needs a human, you talk to a human. When it doesn't, the system handles it.

> _Section header is a direct mirror of §4's. "What you log into" / "What we run" reads as a clean two-column mental model even though the design will likely stack them. The opening line ("most agencies sell you a list of services — we treat it as the work") is the differentiator restated for the visitor who arrived directly at `/how-it-works` from search and didn't read the homepage differentiator block. Closing line — Vonnegut warmth — closes the agency-vs-software loop that runs through the whole page._

## Rationale on the Choices

- **Hero matches the spec.** "One team. One platform. One monthly price." is the locked frame from `site-architecture.md` §5.2 and reads as a three-beat plainspoken declarative — the brand's house cadence. The sub does the differentiator work ("you run the business, we run the marketing") and sets up the page promise ("here's exactly how that works").
- **Page flow mirrors the buyer's question:** what do I get? (the three steps) → what gets set up? (every account) → what do I have to touch? (what you use) → what do they actually do? (what we run) → so what now? (pick / ask / scope). Each section advances exactly one argument.
- **§3 and §4 directly counter the two largest persona objections.** §3 handles "what about A2P 10DLC, Twilio, all that compliance stuff" without using either acronym. §4 handles "I don't want to learn another platform" by naming exactly what they will use — three things — and explicitly listing what they won't have to do.
- **§5 closes the agency-vs-software loop.** Section header mirrors §4's, framing the page as a two-column mental model (you use / we run) that's the entire managed-system pitch.
- **No HighLevel, no A2P 10DLC, no Twilio brand names.** All three translate per the glossary in `product-marketing-context.md` — "our platform," "SMS carrier registration," "a dedicated business phone number."
- **No unverified stats.** Outcomes are stated as direction ("phones get answered faster, reviews come in steadier, no-shows drop") not as percentages.
- **No citrus puns.** Confirmed.
- **"Tier" never used.** "System" throughout per the v3.0 rename.
- **CTAs are action-specific.** "See pricing," "Book Free Consult," "Calculate my missed-call revenue" — never "Get started," "Learn more," "Submit."
- **CTA label discipline.** "Book Free Consult" is the locked label from arch §0.4 — used verbatim in the right card and any header/footer CTA references.
