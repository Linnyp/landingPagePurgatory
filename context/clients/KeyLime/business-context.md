## 1. The Business

- **Business name:** KeyLime Marketing
- **Slug:** keylime
- **One-line description:** A managed digital marketing system for local service businesses — one team, one platform, one monthly price — combining replicable platform features with specialized work where it counts.
- **Industry / category:** B2B services — service/SaaS hybrid digital marketing for local SMBs in home services and beauty/personal services
- **Business model:** Service-and-system hybrid. Three tiered monthly subscriptions (Foundation $99, Growth $195, Expansion $495) bundle platform solutions and, at higher tiers, specialized work. A one-time $200 setup fee covers onboarding and the gameplan deliverable. Add-on solutions and custom-quoted specialized work (SEO programs, paid ads, custom website builds) extend any tier when the business is ready. Replicable HighLevel-powered platform features form the backbone of every account; specialized work is layered on top where it produces outsized returns.
- **Founded / years in business:** Founder has been operating in digital marketing and web development since 2022. The agency formalized in early 2026, transitioned in mid-2026 to the KeyLime Marketing brand alongside the shift to a service/SaaS hybrid model.
- **Geography / service area:** Built to serve local service businesses nationwide. Initial sales and onboarding push is concentrated in Southwest Florida — Lee, Collier, and Charlotte counties — where the team has direct market knowledge and can meet operators in person. The system itself is geography-independent and ready to deploy in any U.S. market.
- **Company size:** Small core team supported by vetted specialist contractors for design, content, link building, ad operations, and custom development. Built to scale from contractor-supported delivery into dedicated in-house roles and, eventually, specialized service-line teams without changing the client-facing experience.
- **Website (current):** None yet. New domain to be selected and registered ahead of the public launch. No public-facing presence until the website ships.
- **Brand archetype:** The Everyman. KeyLime presents as a useful, plain-spoken neighbor — confident without being arrogant, expert without being condescending, modern without being cold. The agency a regular business owner would feel comfortable hiring the way they'd hire a trusted contractor.
- **Notes:** Core positioning rests on five pillars: a complete managed system rather than a checklist of services, service/SaaS hybrid economics powered by HighLevel, industry focus on home services and beauty, plain-English communication that solves owner pain points rather than selling features, and a tier structure built to grow and contract with the business. Specialized work (SEO, paid ads, custom websites) is layered on the platform backbone where it produces outsized returns — KeyLime is not a SaaS product the client buys, learns, and operates. All differentiators must be communicated in plain language consistent with the Everyman archetype. Never lead with technology or acronyms; lead with the problem the owner is trying to fix.

## 2. The Offer

### Primary offer

A managed digital marketing system delivered at one of three tiers. Each tier is a complete, working system the day it goes live, designed to be added to as the business grows.

- **Foundation — $99/month.** The minimum viable marketing system. For new businesses, tight-budget operators, or established businesses that want a core system before committing to more. Includes a site-builder website (1 landing page), Lead Pipeline (CRM), Reputation Management, Unified Inbox, dedicated Twilio number, A2P 10DLC registration, HighLevel sub-account, and baseline workflows.
- **Growth — $195/month.** The full lead-generation engine. For established operators ready to consistently generate, capture, and convert leads. Adds Speed-to-Lead (missed-call text-back, form auto-response, pre-qualification), Booking, Chatbots, Appointment Reminders, Birthday Campaigns, SEO Foundation, and a 3–5 page site-builder website. The most common starting tier for serious operators.
- **Expansion — $495/month.** The full customer lifecycle system. For growing businesses that want a real digital footprint — custom website, content marketing, and ongoing local search optimization on top of the lead engine. Adds a custom Webflow website (5–10 pages), monthly blog content, Lead Nurture sequences, Rewards/loyalty system, GBP management, and quarterly Database Reactivation campaigns.

Underneath the tiers, the system is made up of twelve platform solutions (Lead Pipeline, Speed-to-Lead, Lead Nurture, Reputation Management, Rewards, Birthday Campaigns, Chatbots, Booking, Unified Inbox, Appointment Reminders, Voice Agents, Database Reactivation) and three specialized solutions (SEO, Google & Meta Ads, custom Websites). Different solutions are included in different tiers; any solution not included can be added on.

### Billing & contract terms

- **Setup fee billed once at engagement start.** Monthly subscription begins when onboarding kicks off; the system goes live within the 4–8 week onboarding window.
- **Tiers are month-to-month.** No long-term lock-in on the core subscription. Clients can move up or down as business circumstances change — the same sub-account, contacts, and configured workflows carry over.
- **Custom specialized work is scoped per engagement.** Paid ads, larger SEO programs, and custom website projects are quoted and contracted separately with terms that fit the scope.
- The pricing principle: clients should always know what something costs before they buy it, without being buried in dozens of line items. Tier prices are published openly. Where price depends meaningfully on scope, KeyLime quotes rather than guesses.

### Primary differentiators

1. **A complete system, not a checklist of services.** Most local marketing buyers are asked to assemble a stack — SEO from one vendor, ads from another, a CRM from a third, a review tool from a fourth, a website from a fifth. KeyLime delivers one managed system at one monthly price.
2. **Service/SaaS hybrid economics.** Replicable platform features powered by HighLevel mean real marketing infrastructure is affordable for a $300K-revenue business, not just a $5M one. Lower-tier clients get a real working system, not a stripped-down demo.
3. **Industry focus.** Home services and beauty/personal services share the operational shape KeyLime is built for. Industry-specific workflows, language, integrations, and benchmarks are built in — not adapted from a generic template.
4. **Plain-English communication.** We explain what we do in terms of the problems we solve. Owners don't need to learn what A2P 10DLC is or how attribution modeling works — they need their phones to ring more, their no-shows to drop, and their reviews to keep coming in.
5. **Built for growth, both directions.** The same client can start at Foundation, expand to Expansion with added paid ads, and contract back down if conditions change — all on the same platform, with the same team, without rebuilding.

### Guarantee / risk reversal

- **Layered, no-commitment entry points.** A prospect can quantify their own missed-call revenue with the calculator or download an industry field report before ever booking a call. The discovery call is reserved for prospects who already understand the problem and want to scope the right tier.
- **Month-to-month tiers.** No long-term lock-in on the core subscription. The system grows and contracts with the business.
- **Full onboarding included in the setup fee.** Discovery, platform setup, the written gameplan, and the workflow build are all covered. The system is live and working within 4–8 weeks, not a year from now.

### Add-on solutions and specialized work

Beyond the tier inclusions, additional capabilities can be layered onto any engagement:

- **Published add-ons (platform solutions not in tier):** Voice Agents, Lead Nurture, Rewards, Database Reactivation (one-time campaign), GMB management on lower tiers, additional automations and chatbots.
- **Custom-quoted specialized work:** Full SEO programs, ongoing content beyond what's bundled, Google and Meta ads management, custom Webflow builds beyond included pages, large bespoke workflow projects.

### Current launch status

Brand transition in progress from the prior agency model to KeyLime Marketing. Standardizing tier deliverables and onboarding playbooks. Onboarding the first 5–10 paying clients across the three tiers, concentrated in Southwest Florida. Public site and brand identity not yet live.

## 3. The Audience

- **Primary target customer:** Owners and operators of local service businesses in two industries:
  - **Home services.** Remodeling, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, garage doors, epoxy and flooring, pest control, landscaping, pressure washing, pool service, and similar trades.
  - **Beauty and personal services.** Salons, barbershops, med spas, lash and brow studios, nail salons, massage and body work, and similar appointment-based personal services.

  Both share the operational shape KeyLime is built for: repeat customer relationships, appointment-driven revenue, reliance on local reputation, and a strong need for fast lead response. Typical client size $300K–$5M in annual revenue. Established or growing operators who know their trade but don't have the time, team, or interest to manage a marketing stack themselves. Initial sales focus is Southwest Florida (Lee, Collier, Charlotte counties); the system serves qualifying businesses nationwide.

- **Secondary audience:** Operators who have tried piecing together SaaS tools (separate CRM, separate review tool, separate booking software, separate texting platform) and are tired of the patchwork. Also operators who have hired a generalist local agency and were underwhelmed by results — looking for a managed system that actually runs rather than a list of services on an invoice.
- **Most painful problem:** Leads slip through the cracks. The phone rings while they're on a job and the lead calls the next business on the list. Reviews are stale or scattered. No-shows kill the calendar. Old quotes never get followed up on. Customer messages are spread across five different inboxes. They know they're losing business to better-positioned competitors but don't have the time, in-house staff, or technical know-how to fix it — and they don't trust most agencies to fix it for them.
- **Current alternative:** Doing marketing themselves (poorly), relying on word-of-mouth and referrals alone, working with a generalist local agency that ships template sites and hits-or-misses on SEO, or paying for a stack of disconnected SaaS tools they never fully use.
- **Desired outcome:** A phone that rings more, fewer leads slipping through, fewer no-shows, a steady stream of reviews, repeat customers coming back, and a digital presence that actually reflects the quality of their business — all without becoming a marketer themselves.
- **Biggest objection to buying:** Cost sensitivity (SMB budgets are tight), skepticism from past bad agency experiences ("I've been burned before"), uncertainty about ROI timelines on SEO and content, and the fear that they'll be sold something they can't operate. The "you don't have to learn the platform — we run it" message has to land clearly.
- **Sophistication level:** Warm. They know they need better marketing. Most are aware AI exists but don't understand how it applies to their business. They likely don't know KeyLime specifically — discovery happens through search, referrals, local networking, and eventually targeted paid campaigns.
- **Where they come from:** Google search (e.g., "marketing for HVAC", "salon marketing", "local SEO Naples"), Google Business Profile discovery, referrals from existing clients, local networking and chamber events in the early SWFL phase, and — as the brand scales — targeted Meta and Google ads.

## 5. Visual Preferences

### Brand archetype

**The Everyman.** KeyLime presents as relatable, grounded, and trustworthy — not as a technical wizard, a disruptor, or a tech-bro. The goal is "one of us" energy: the agency a local business owner would feel comfortable hiring the way they'd hire a trusted contractor. Warmth over polish, clarity over cleverness, plainspoken confidence over jargon.

### Brand persona

A useful, plain-spoken neighbor who happens to be very good at marketing. Small business owners in home services and beauty don't want to feel dumb, don't want to be talked down to, and don't want to be impressed by jargon they can't decode. The KeyLime brand should feel like a smart, competent friend — the kind of person who explains things plainly because they respect you, not because they think you're simple. ## 6. Page Goals & Constraints

### Conversion strategy

The site is structured around a layered conversion path rather than a single "book a call" funnel. Different pages serve different stages of buyer intent, and each has its own primary CTA. The shape of the funnel from top to bottom is:

1. **Diagnostic calculators** (low commitment, no email required to start). Pattern-break the "another agency pitch" reflex by turning the page into a useful tool. The prospect quantifies their own problem before KeyLime says a word about itself.
2. **Field reports** (email-gated downloads). Capture the "interested but not ready" visitor into the nurture sequence.
3. **Discovery call** (Calendly). The high-intent endpoint for prospects who already understand the problem and want to scope the right tier.

Every page has a single primary CTA tuned to its intent stage. The discovery-call CTA is persistent in the header and footer on every page, but it is not the primary CTA on most pages — using it everywhere would dull it and pattern-match to every other local agency.

### Page-by-page primary CTA map

| Page                                            | Primary CTA                                | Secondary                |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------ |
| Homepage hero                                   | Missed-call revenue calculator             | "See pricing" text link  |
| Tier pages (Foundation / Growth / Expansion)    | "Book a discovery call"                    | Calculator link          |
| Industry pages (HVAC, salon, etc.)              | Industry-matched calculator                | "See pricing"            |
| Comparison pages ("KeyLime + Mindbody", etc.)   | "Calculate your stack cost"                | "Book a call"            |
| Field report pages                              | "Download the report" (email-gated)        | Newsletter signup        |
| Contact / Book a Call                           | Calendly embed                             | —                        |

### Lead magnets

KeyLime runs a family of lead magnets rather than a single download, each tied to a specific stage of intent:

- **Missed-call revenue calculator** (primary, homepage). A 60-second, three-input calculator that shows the prospect what missed calls are costing them each month. The hero CTA is the calculator entry — not a pointer to it. Industry-tuned variants live on each industry page (HVAC, salon, plumbing, etc.) and use vertical-specific defaults, benchmarks, and labels.
- **Stack cost calculator** (comparison pages). On pages like "KeyLime + Mindbody," "KeyLime + Housecall Pro," "KeyLime + Vagaro," the prospect tallies what their current stack of point tools costs each month and compares it to KeyLime's all-in price.
- **Field reports** (email-gated). Short, opinionated industry reports — e.g., "Why local HVAC companies lose 30% of their inbound calls," "The real ROI of review automation in beauty." Downloaded via email gate; the email enters a nurture sequence.

The legacy "Free Digital Presence Audit" lead magnet is replaced by this layered funnel. Audit-style language ("get a free audit," "we'll review your digital presence") should not appear in CTAs going forward — it pattern-matches to every other local agency and underperforms the calculator on the homepage.

### Hero direction (loss-led)

The homepage hero is the moment a prospect either pattern-matches KeyLime to "another agency" or registers it as something different. The calculator is the pattern-break, so the hero CTA must be the calculator entry — not a generic "Learn more" or "Book a call" with the calculator buried below the fold.

Reference frame for the hero:

- **H1:** See what missed calls are costing your business.
- **Sub:** Local home services and beauty operators lose thousands a month to leads that called the next business on the list. Punch in three numbers — get yours in 60 seconds.
- **Primary button:** Calculate my missed-call revenue
- **Secondary text link:** See how the system works

Why this framing: loss aversion is sharper than gain framing for the established-operator profile that's been burned by past agencies. The "three numbers / 60 seconds" guardrail defuses the "this is going to be a sales trap" reflex. The button is action-specific, not generic. Avoid generic "Get started," "Get a free audit," or "Book a call" CTAs in the hero.

### Secondary conversion events

Behind the page-specific primary CTAs, the following events are tracked across the site:

- **Calendly bookings.** High-intent endpoint, reachable from every page via persistent header/footer CTA.
- **Calculator completions.** Even when the prospect doesn't book, a completed calculator captures an email at the result screen and enters the nurture sequence.
- **Field report downloads.** Always email-gated; entry into the nurture sequence.
- **Quote requests.** Form-based capture for custom-scoped specialized work (custom website, full SEO program, paid ads).

### Required sections

- Homepage with the loss-led hero and the missed-call revenue calculator as the primary above-the-fold action
- Tier pages for Foundation, Growth, and Expansion with published pricing and clear inclusions; primary CTA is "Book a discovery call"
- Solutions / capabilities section covering the twelve platform solutions and three specialized solutions, organized so owners can find the pain point they're trying to solve
- Industry pages for home services and beauty/personal services with vertical-specific language, examples, and industry-tuned calculator variants
- Comparison pages ("KeyLime + Mindbody," "KeyLime + Housecall Pro," "KeyLime + Vagaro," and similar) with the stack cost calculator as the primary CTA
- Field report landing pages with email-gated downloads as the primary CTA and newsletter signup as the secondary
- About page that establishes team credibility and the managed-system story
- Case studies / results page (placeholder structure now, populated as results mature)
- Contact / Book a Call page with embedded Calendly
- Quote request flow for custom-scoped specialized work

### Excluded sections

No standalone "AI services" page — AI is a delivery method woven through every service, not a product. No e-commerce. No client login portal on the marketing site (client login lives inside the HighLevel sub-account). No "Free Digital Presence Audit" landing page — replaced by the calculator and field report layered funnel. No full price list for every add-on and specialized service; published pricing is on tiers and clearly labeled add-ons, with quote requests for everything else.

### Legal requirements

Standard privacy policy and terms of service. Cookie consent banner. A2P 10DLC and SMS consent disclosures where applicable on lead capture forms and calculator email gates. No industry-specific regulatory requirements anticipated.

## 7. Tone & Copy Guidance

> **Writing voice reference:** The full writing voice specification — including reference writers, distilled sentence/vocabulary/tone/structure rules, before-and-after copy examples, and a pre-submission checklist — will be documented in **`writing-voice.md`** in this same directory. Any AI agent writing copy for KeyLime must load and follow `writing-voice.md` once available. The summary below is supplementary context.

- **Voice adjectives:** Direct, knowledgeable, approachable, plainspoken, no-BS, warm, useful.
- **Formality level:** 2.5 out of 5 — professional but conversational. Speaks like a knowledgeable friend or trusted neighbor, never like a corporate brochure. Easy for non-technical business owners to follow without ever feeling talked down to.
- **Humor:** Subtle dry wit. Confident but never trying too hard. No memes, no forced casualness, no "fellow kids" energy. No citrus puns in body copy; the brand is named KeyLime, it doesn't need to keep telling you it's named KeyLime.
- **Words to avoid:** "Synergy," "leverage" (as a verb in marketing-speak), "disrupt," "guru," "ninja," "rockstar," "revolutionary," "game-changer," "unlock," "supercharge," "next-level," "cutting-edge" (as filler), "solutions" (as filler), "empower," "elevate." Avoid empty hype language and AI-marketing clichés. Avoid technical jargon — A2P 10DLC, attribution, omnichannel, MarTech stack — unless it's immediately translated into plain language about what it does for the business.
- **Existing slogans / taglines:** None. To be defined after site copy is drafted.
- **Key messages to include** (all phrased for the Everyman persona — plain, confident, outcome-focused):
  - **One system, one team, one price.** "Stop juggling six tools, five logins, and four invoices. We run the whole thing as one managed system."
  - **You run the business. We run the marketing.** "You don't have to learn a platform, configure a workflow, or chase down a vendor. That's what we're here for."
  - **A working system in weeks, not months.** "Onboarding takes 4 to 8 weeks. By the end, your phones ring faster, your reviews come in steadier, and your no-shows drop."
  - **Right-sized to where the business is today.** "Starting out? Foundation gets you a real working system for $99 a month. Ready for more? Growth and Expansion are there when you are."
  - **Industry-specific, not generic.** "We build for home services and beauty operators because that's what our system is tuned for — workflows, language, and benchmarks that fit how you actually work."
  - **Fair pricing, no runaround.** "Tier prices are on the site. Where price depends on scope — a custom website, a real SEO program, an ad budget — we quote you instead of guessing."
- **CTA copy direction.** Primary CTAs across the site are action-specific and outcome-named, not generic. "Calculate my missed-call revenue" beats "Get started." "Download the report" beats "Submit." Loss-framed copy outperforms gain-framed copy in the hero ("See what missed calls are costing your business" over "Grow your revenue with KeyLime") because the target audience is more motivated by avoiding waste than by chasing upside. Reserve "Book a discovery call" for tier pages and the contact page — using it on every page dulls it and pattern-matches to every other local agency.
