# **Executive Summary**

This document presents six research-backed user personas representing the primary customer and prospect segments for KeyLime Marketing's managed digital marketing system. Each persona is built from the intersection of the KeyLime Business & Product Documentation v4.0, current industry research from authoritative sources (LocaliQ, Mordor Intelligence, ServiceTitan, WebFX, Wix/VistaPrint, BDR, HIRI, Mindbody, Boulevard, Professional Beauty Association), and Southwest Florida regional market context.

The personas are designed to directly support KeyLime's service/SaaS hybrid model by mapping each customer archetype to a starting tier (Foundation $99 / Growth $195 / Expansion $495), identifying their natural growth path through add-on solutions and custom-quoted specialized work (SEO, paid ads, custom Webflow), and quantifying estimated monthly revenue per persona. Together, these personas cover the operational shape KeyLime is built for: appointment-driven local service businesses from $50K to $10M+ in revenue, 1–50+ employees, across home services (HVAC, plumbing, roofing, remodeling, landscaping, pest control, garage doors, flooring, pool service) and beauty/personal services (salons, barbershops, med spas, lash and brow studios, nail salons, massage and body work).

Three industries-spanning archetypes (the solo startup, the office-manager champion, and the multi-location operator) sit alongside three industry-anchored archetypes (one home services owner-operator, one home services legacy operator, and one beauty multi-station owner). The matrix at the back of this document maps each persona to entry tier, expansion path, add-on attach opportunities, and estimated MRR.

**PERSONA 1**

# **Mike Hernandez**

**"The Scaling Contractor" —** HVAC / Plumbing / Electrical Business Owner

*"I miss a call and the next one on the Google list gets the job. I don't even know how much business I'm losing. I just want the phone to ring with good leads — and I want to actually pick up."*

  **DEMOGRAPHICS & FIRMOGRAPHICS**

| Attribute | Detail |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Age** | 38–52 |
| **Gender** | Male (predominantly) |
| **Location** | Cape Coral / Fort Myers metro, Lee County FL — replicable nationally |
| **Education** | Trade school or some college; licensed contractor |
| **Household Income** | $120K–$250K personal; business revenue $750K–$3M |
| **Business Size** | 8–25 employees (techs, dispatchers, office staff) |
| **Business Age** | 5–15 years established |
| **Tech Proficiency** | Low-to-moderate; uses smartphone heavily, desktop for QuickBooks or ServiceTitan |

  **PSYCHOGRAPHIC PROFILE**

Mike is intensely proud of the business he built from his truck. He measures success by truck count and revenue growth. He distrusts marketing agencies because he has been burned before — paying $1,500/month for SEO with nothing to show for it. He respects people who speak in plain language and show measurable results. He is time-poor, spending 10+ hours a day managing crews, handling escalations, and quoting jobs. His wife or office manager currently handles social media sporadically. He is competitive and watches what his rivals are doing on Google, but feels behind. He doesn't want to learn another platform — he wants the platform run *for* him.

  **GOALS & MOTIVATIONS**

* Stop losing missed-call jobs to whoever's listed next on Google

* Increase inbound call volume by 30–50% without proportionally increasing ad spend

* Dominate the Google Local Pack / Maps for his primary service area

* Build a review engine that consistently generates 5-star reviews

* Systematize lead follow-up so no opportunity falls through the cracks

* Reduce dependency on Angi / HomeAdvisor / shared-lead platforms

  **PAIN POINTS & FRUSTRATIONS**

* Misses 20–40% of calls during the day while on jobs; those leads call the next business and book elsewhere

* Has paid agencies in the past with unclear ROI and no data transparency

* Leads from third-party platforms are expensive and often shared with 3–5 competitors

* No CRM — leads tracked in spreadsheets, sticky notes, or dispatcher memory

* Reviews are stagnant at 60–80 while competitors have 200+

* Website is outdated (built 5+ years ago), not mobile-optimized, slow load times

* No call tracking; cannot attribute which marketing efforts generate revenue

* No automated follow-up on old quotes — thousands in lifetime value left on the table

  **BUYER JOURNEY & DISCOVERY**

Mike typically enters via a Google search for marketing help ("HVAC marketing," "plumbing leads near me," "missed call texting"), a referral from another contractor, or a targeted Meta/Google ad. He responds best to case studies and concrete numbers ("missed-call text-back recovered $14K in booked jobs in the first 30 days"). His evaluation process is short — a discovery call, maybe one follow-up. He wants to know the price, the timeline, and what he'll see in 90 days. He is most likely to enter at the **Growth tier ($195/mo)** because Speed-to-Lead (missed-call text-back, form auto-response, pre-qualification) directly solves his most painful problem, and SEO Foundation starts the long-tail of Google visibility. Once Growth is producing, he's a strong candidate for custom-quoted Google Ads management as the next layer.

  **COMMON OBJECTIONS**

* "I've been burned by agencies before — how are you different?"

* "I don't have time to learn a new platform or deal with another login."

* "Can you just handle everything? I don't want homework."

* "How quickly will I see results? I need leads NOW, not in 6 months."

* "I already use ServiceTitan / Housecall Pro — am I switching off that?"

  **KEYLIME FIT & REVENUE OPPORTUNITY**

Mike is the archetypal **Growth tier** client. Missed-call text-back, online booking, and review automation start producing the first month — many Mike-type accounts cover the subscription on a single recovered job. As trust builds, the natural expansion is adding **custom-quoted Google Ads management** (Local Services Ads + Google Search + Performance Max) and, eventually, moving up to **Expansion** for a custom Webflow site and content marketing once organic traction is in motion. KeyLime sits *around* his existing field service software (ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber) — it doesn't replace it. The platform's "we run it for you" framing directly addresses his past trust issues with agencies.

| **Likely 12-month state** | Growth + Ads management ($800–$2,000/mo custom-quoted) |

* No-shows and late cancels eat real revenue — at $150–$300 per appointment, every no-show stings

* Reviews trickle in; competitors with newer studios have 400+ reviews vs. her 90

* Birthday and loyalty marketing exist only as good intentions — she doesn't have time to set it up

* Her website was built 2 years ago by a friend's nephew; she can't update it and it doesn't book appointments

* Past lapsed clients sit in Mindbody untouched; she knows there's money there but can't get to it

She typically enters at **Growth ($195/mo)** with eyes on adding **Rewards** (loyalty) as a published add-on early, then upgrades to **Expansion ($495/mo)** within 6–12 months once she sees the system working and wants the custom Webflow site, GBP management, and quarterly Database Reactivation. She's not a candidate for custom-scoped Google Ads management — Meta Ads (Instagram) is the higher-fit specialized work for beauty.

  **COMMON OBJECTIONS**

* "I already use Mindbody / Boulevard — does this replace it or work alongside?"

* "Do my stylists/technicians have to learn anything new?"

* "Can the reviews go to Google AND my Yelp / Instagram?"

* "How do you handle booking deposits and no-show fees? Mindbody does that today."

* "What about HIPAA stuff for the med spa side?" (if med spa)

  **KEYLIME FIT & REVENUE OPPORTUNITY**

Lena is the archetypal **beauty Growth-to-Expansion** client. KeyLime sits *around* Mindbody/Boulevard/Vagaro — these tools stay; KeyLime layers on the missed-call texting, review automation, unified inbox, birthday campaigns, and (on Expansion) loyalty/rewards and database reactivation. The pitch lands hard on tool consolidation math: her current SaaS spend often matches or exceeds Growth tier on its own. Custom-quoted **Meta Ads management** is a strong specialized-work attach for beauty (Instagram is where her clients live). Multi-station beauty operators are excellent candidates for Rewards-driven retention metrics that become case-study material.

# **Research Methodology & Sources**

These personas were constructed using a triangulated research approach combining four data categories:

**1\. Business Context Analysis**

Cross-referenced with the KeyLime business-context.md positioning document.

**2\. Industry & Market Research — Home Services**

* LocaliQ 2025 and 2026 Small Business Marketing Trends Reports (300–730+ SMB survey respondents): budget trends, channel preferences, top challenges, AI adoption rates

* Home services industry data from Mordor Intelligence, BDR, ServiceTitan, WebFX, and Comrade Digital: market sizing ($650B–$750B), growth rates (HVAC 5.5% CAGR, plumbing 5.42%), CPL benchmarks, conversion rates, and technology adoption trends

* HIRI Home Services Study: homeowner spending segments (affluent young, affluent middle-aged, middle-income seniors, low-income seniors), hiring preferences, and service category spending

* CEO GPS 500 Key Digital Marketing Statistics for 2026: SMB acquisition challenges (59% cite new customers as top challenge), review impact (90% read before purchasing), and SEO adoption gaps (61% have not invested)

**3\. Industry & Market Research — Beauty / Personal Services**

* Professional Beauty Association industry reports: salon and spa market sizing, average ticket and lifetime value benchmarks, retention and rebooking rates

* Mindbody and Boulevard industry data: beauty and wellness booking behavior, no-show rates (industry average 15–20%), client lifetime value, and rebooking cadence

* American Med Spa Association data: med spa growth rates, average ticket size, repeat visit cadence, and reputation management dynamics

* Wix/VistaPrint 2026 SMB Marketing Statistics Report (1,000 SBOs + 1,000 consumers surveyed): motivations, AI usage, offline/online balance, generational preferences

**4\. Regional Context (Initial Sales Concentration)**

* Southwest Florida Economic Development Alliance, Lee County Economic Development Office, and Cape Coral CRA data on local business landscape and incentive programs

* Post-Hurricane Ian recovery dynamics and their impact on home services demand in Lee / Collier / Charlotte counties

* SWFL seasonal tourism patterns and their effect on appointment-based personal services revenue cycles

* National replication notes: each persona's profile is portable to any U.S. market with the same operational shape — peak season and competitive density vary, but the underlying pain points (missed calls, no-shows, fragmented tool stacks, stale reviews) are universal.
